About the programme

Learning how to learn, not just getting the answer.

Peer Coaching is a student-run programme at UWCSEA. Trained peer coaches run regular sessions in the subjects they know well — free, on campus, and built around study habits that last.

Why it exists

Private tuition in Singapore is expensive and not everyone can access it. Peer Coaching exists so that a student who is struggling can get consistent, knowledgeable help from someone who sat the same courses recently — without a price tag. That access-for-everyone idea is the whole point of the programme.

What a session looks like

It is not a lecture. A typical session runs 30–40 minutes and is driven by what you bring:

  • Working through problems together, not watching someone solve them.
  • Going over your past papers and mark schemes.
  • Planning essays or a revision timetable.
  • Sharing the specific study habits that worked for your coach.

Most pairs meet once or twice a week, at lunch or in a shared study period, for the length of a term. You and your coach agree the schedule.

Who the coaches are

Every coach is a UWCSEA student — usually in Grade 11 or 12 — with a strong record in their subject (typically a 6 or 7 at their current level), a teacher recommendation, and a short training session on how to run a coaching meeting. Meet the coaches →

Questions

Frequently asked

Draft Answers to confirm with coordinators.

How do I sign up for coaching?

Fill in the student sign-up form linked in the navigation. You’ll give your subject, current level and free periods. A coordinator matches you with a coach within about a week.

How do I become a peer coach?

Use the “Become a peer coach” form. You’ll usually need a 6 or 7 in the subject at your current level and a teacher recommendation. Coaches get a short training session first.

Does it really cost nothing?

Nothing. Peer Coaching is completely free — that is the point of the programme.

How much time does it take?

Most pairs meet once or twice a week for 30–40 minutes, usually at lunch or in a shared study period.

What if the match isn’t right?

Tell a coordinator. Re-matching is normal and no one takes it personally — the right fit matters more than the first pairing.

Start with one session.

You don’t have to commit to a term. Sign up, try a session, see if it helps.
